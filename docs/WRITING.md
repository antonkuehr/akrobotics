# Perspective and tense

All future website text and revisions must follow these rules. Apply them by the purpose of each passage, rather than imposing one perspective or tense on the whole site. Use British English and preserve the factual scope in [Content sources](CONTENT.md).

## Rules by text type

| Text type                                                    | Perspective                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Tense or form                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Example                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| About me, motivation and personal interests                  | First person singular: I, me, my. Inclusive “we” is appropriate when explicitly referring to people in general.                                                                                                             | Present for current beliefs, interests and activities; past for earlier events or beliefs; present perfect for experience connected to the present.                                                                 | “I believe…”; “When I started…”; “This belief has guided…”                                                               |
| Education, jobs, volunteering and awards                     | Implied first person for concise CV entries; explicit “my” is acceptable inside a longer description. Use neutral language when explaining an institution or programme.                                                     | Past simple for completed responsibilities and awards; present for continuing responsibilities; noun phrases for qualifications and role labels.                                                                    | “Developed hardware…”; “Occasionally volunteer…”; “MSc Robotics”.                                                        |
| Completed project narratives and individual contributions    | Explicit I for Anton's work; we/our for documented shared work. Name collaborators where appropriate.                                                                                                                       | Past simple for work, decisions, testing and observed results. Past perfect is appropriate for events preceding a stated endpoint, such as handover.                                                                | “I compared…”; “We tested…”; “At handover, we had not…”                                                                  |
| Ongoing project work                                         | I for individual work; we only for supported team work; neutral language for the wider project.                                                                                                                             | Present or present continuous for current responsibilities and investigations; past for completed milestones within that work.                                                                                      | “I am recruiting…”; “The work investigates…”; “I initiated the skin team…”                                               |
| Objectives, proposals and future work                        | Preserve the actual individual or team responsible; neutral objectives are also acceptable.                                                                                                                                 | Explicit aims or qualified future/conditional wording. Use “will” for intended actions only when supported, never to promise an unverified result. Completed projects describe proposals as proposals at that time. | “The objectives are…”; “could be integrated…”; “Proposed changes included…”                                              |
| Technical explanations, equations and source interpretation  | Neutral, with the component, method, diagram or report as the subject.                                                                                                                                                      | Present for how the documented design works, what a diagram shows, mathematical relationships and what a source states. Past for what was built or measured in a particular test.                                   | “The tracks form an 8 × 8 matrix”; “The prototype detected…”                                                             |
| Headings, project leads, cards, facts, skills and captions   | Neutral noun phrases by default. A heading such as “My contribution” may identify the personal section.                                                                                                                     | Descriptive headings rather than instructions. Completed summaries use noun phrases or past descriptions; active investigation may use a present participle. Captions may describe what is visible in the present.  | “Dataset correction and detector evaluation”; “Prototype built and evaluated”; “Investigating scalable tactile sensing…” |
| Navigation, buttons, contact invitations and algorithm steps | Address the visitor directly for actions; state algorithm operations directly without attributing them to the reader.                                                                                                       | Imperative action labels and present status messages. These are intentional exceptions to descriptive case-study headings.                                                                                          | “View certificate”; “Get in touch”; “Initialise”; “Set arm to rest position”.                                            |
| Metadata and social previews                                 | Third person or a neutral project description so a shared link identifies Anton and the project without relying on the surrounding page. Action-led descriptions such as “Explore…” are acceptable for visitor invitations. | Present for current profile information or a documented operating principle; past or completed noun phrases for finished work; explicit ongoing wording for active work.                                            | “Anton Kühr is an MSc Robotics student…”; “The project compared…”                                                        |

## Applying the rules

- Switching tense within a paragraph is correct when the time reference or subject changes. “I initiated the team and am recruiting members” distinguishes an earlier milestone from current work. “We tested the prototype. The diagram shows the setup” distinguishes an experiment from the page's explanation.
- “We” must not absorb Anton's individual contribution or imply that a team result was achieved individually. Conversely, do not turn shared work into “I” for stylistic consistency.
- Dates and stated status determine the narrative tense. A past role can still include a present description of the employer or programme. A finished project can still explain its documented operating principle in the present; this does not imply continued deployment or new validation.
- Facts, qualifications, skills and role labels do not need full sentences. “Member of a student association…” is a valid CV fragment when the dated entry establishes that the membership ended.
- State observed results in the past. Mathematical interpretations and evidence limits can remain in the present: “WER fell…” followed by “This is a 9.3% relative reduction” does not imply a second experiment.
- Keep exact quotations, original titles, certificates, references and reports faithful to their sources. Their wording is not rewritten to impose the website's house style.
- When new evidence changes a project's status, update its card, lead, dates, narrative and metadata together. Do not silently turn objectives into achievements.
