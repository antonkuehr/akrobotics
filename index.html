01 / About me

I’m a robotics master’s student at TU Delft, building on a BSc in Mechanical Engineering – Product Engineering and Context (MPEC) at TH Köln.

What draws me to robotics is the combination of mechanics, sensing, software and control needed to make machines understand and interact with the world around them. I’m particularly interested in robotic perception: enabling robots to see, hear and feel their environment.

My projects have explored these different senses through computer vision for an autonomous agricultural robot, a tactile sensing prototype, and auditory perception in my bachelor’s thesis on the social robot Furhat. During my internship at Furhat Robotics in Stockholm, I worked across hardware and software prototyping, including the 360° Furhat and the Furpack project. Explore these projects in the portfolio section.

I’m pursuing an MSc in Robotics at TU Delft, expanding my knowledge in areas including machine learning, robot dynamics, control and perception. I also started and now lead the skin team within RSA Project Echo, focused on tactile sensing and protective soft skin for humanoid robots.

MSc Robotics

Delft University of Technology · Delft, Netherlands

Machine learning, robot dynamics, control and perception.

Details

TU Delft’s two-year, 120-ECTS MSc Robotics connects mechanical engineering with artificial intelligence to develop intelligent robotic systems. It covers modelling, design, control and validation, with particular attention to how robots learn, interact with people and operate in complex environments.

The first year builds a common foundation through Robot Dynamics and Control, Machine Learning for Robotics, Robot Software Practicals, Machine Perception, Planning and Decision Making, and Human–Robot Interaction. Robot and Society adds ethical and societal perspectives, while a multidisciplinary project brings the technical subjects together. Electives allow students to deepen their interests; Vision and Reflection develops communication, reflective teamwork and professional judgement.

In the second year, students choose an internship or assignment, a joint interdisciplinary project, or further in-depth electives. This is followed by literature research and an MSc thesis, applying the programme’s methods to a complex research or design problem.

BSc Product Engineering and Context

Cologne University of Applied Sciences / TH Köln · Cologne, Germany

Project-based mechanical engineering programme. Thesis grade: 1.0. Final grade: 1.1 (excellent).

Details

Mechanical Engineering – Product Engineering and Context (MPEC) is a seven-semester, 210-ECTS bachelor’s programme at TH Köln. It combines mechanical engineering, robotics and computing through the development of mechatronic products and systems, considering user needs, sustainability and the social, economic and ethical context of engineering decisions.

Learning centres on realistic, interdisciplinary team projects from the first semester. Coaches support students in acquiring the technical knowledge and problem-solving methods needed for each challenge. During the second and third semesters, teams develop a product while taking increasing responsibility for project organisation, collaboration and independent learning.

Project topics and elective modules in the fourth and fifth semesters allow students to shape their own focus and explore perspectives beyond engineering. The sixth semester offers an industry placement, study abroad or a start-up project; the seventh is devoted to the bachelor’s thesis.
