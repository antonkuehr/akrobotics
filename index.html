01 / About me

When I started my bachelor's degree in 2022, ChatGPT didn't exist yet, and I thought humanoid robots only existed in movies. Just a few years later, I can hardly imagine what studying robotics without AI looks like, and full-sized humanoids apparently do backflips now. Watching these developments unfold fascinates me like a little kid who can't wait to be part of it.

At the same time, there's something unsettling about humanity's race to build machines in our own image. Yet, with ageing populations and labour shortages, I believe humanoids will have an important role to play in our society. I want to help shape that future responsibly.

For robots to truly become part of our world, I believe they need to perceive it as we do: to see, hear, feel, and perhaps even smell and taste. This belief has guided many of my projects, from computer vision for an agricultural robot during my bachelor's studies and auditory perception in my bachelor's thesis at Furhat Robotics to starting the skin team for the humanoid student project Echo at TU Delft.

On this website, I share these projects alongside my education, professional experience and the skills I've developed along the way. If you have questions, opportunities or simply want to connect, feel free to get in touch.

Programme description

TU Delft’s two-year, 120-ECTS MSc Robotics connects mechanical engineering and artificial intelligence through the modelling, design and control of robotic systems. The first year builds a shared foundation in robot dynamics, machine learning, perception and planning, alongside software practicals, human–robot interaction and a multidisciplinary project. Societal questions and reflective teamwork complement the technical subjects.

My specialisation will take shape through elective choices in the third and fourth quarters (Q3 and Q4) of the first year. The second year offers an internship or assignment, an interdisciplinary project, or further in-depth electives, followed by literature research and an MSc thesis. This structure moves from a common robotics foundation towards applying it to an individual research or design problem.

Details

Programme description

When I began in 2022, TH Köln’s Mechanical Engineering – Product Engineering and Context (MPEC) was a pilot programme exploring a project-based approach to engineering education. The seven-semester, 210-ECTS degree connects mechanical engineering and computing with product development, considering user needs and the social, economic and environmental context of design decisions.

The first three semesters centre on integrated Engineering Office projects, combining mathematics, mechanics, materials and programming with building mechatronic systems. Coaches support the technical learning as teams take increasing responsibility for design and project organisation. Project topics and electives in the fourth and fifth semesters allow an individual focus, followed by an industry, study-abroad or start-up semester and the bachelor’s thesis.

BSc programme

Robotics focus

In the fourth and fifth semesters, I used the choice of projects and electives to focus on robotics. My electives included Human–Machine Interaction and Service and Industry Robotics, connecting how people interact with robots with the development of practical robotic systems.

For Product Engineering I in the fourth semester, I worked on the sensing comparison for the harvesting robot. In Product Engineering II in the fifth semester, I worked on a flexible tactile sensor. These projects brought together mechanical design, sensing and control, and led to my bachelor’s thesis on microphone integration and speech recognition at Furhat Robotics.
